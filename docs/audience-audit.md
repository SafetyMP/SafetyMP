# Can visitors learn enough to make the next decision?

**Audience walkthrough, November 22, 2026.** Each of the ten proposed redesigns gives its operating user a decision to try and its prospective sponsor a reason to compare alternatives. The most revealing moments occur when one fact changes and the easy answer stops working. Examples include a report for the wrong referral, a leave request that HR declines, and a matching liquidity ratio calculated from different dates.

This assessment uses the rendered cases, project fixtures, desktop and mobile checks, and independent walkthroughs of the decision logic. No operators, customers or sponsors took part in a usability study. The findings below describe what the demos let people do and what to observe with participants. The [field guide](demo-field-guide.md) links the cases, screenshots and primary sources behind the audience choices.

## Comparison with the APS case

In APS, a buyer can hold the request fixed, change its release time and trace the consequence. That gives us a concrete test for the other demos. Before a visitor acts, can they inspect the records that matter? Afterward, can they explain the consequence and replay the decision with one fact changed?

The first priority was removing answers from the opening exercise. A visible FAIL in Commerce, a release verdict in HR-ERP, and “Limited return” in Healthcare Data Exchange let a visitor answer without reading the records. The billing case exposed the calculated amount before the accountant reconciled the register. The financial case recommended a handoff immediately after calculating the ratio. SOC-2’s quarter packet labels named the finding before the reviewer saw the inventory. Four other cases needed a stronger link between evidence and action. Asclepius accepted a queue decision without its supporting reference. EHS lacked conflicting field evidence. CorpOS asked an engineer to diagnose billing without a service observation. FidusGate recommended retrieving file history but ended before the investigator could inspect it. The walkthroughs below give a reproducible comparison for each draft.

At 50% signed acceptance, issuing the existing $6,000 draft leaves $6,000 available to bill. Restarting with the same acceptance and voiding the draft leaves $12,000. The audit caught a reset that used to change acceptance at the same time. The revised case isolates the effect of the draft decision. | Ask the accountant to reconcile both amounts and identify the evidence of cash receipt they would still need. Ask the partner whether the measured time saving would cover the pilot if corrections had to be handled manually. |
| **Financial Digital Twin** · Treasury and independent liquidity risk, with the CFO as sponsor | The case shows the calculated shortfall or headroom, leaving the next action to the reviewer. An observation of €92m in assets divided by €92m of projected outflows shows 100%. When the outflow schedule is a day older than the asset observation, documenting the ratio leaves an open evidence exception. A same-date schedule supports a documented boundary-case review. | Give Treasury and risk reviewers two unfamiliar dated schedules with identical ratios. Can they agree who owns the next task and which date matters? Ask the CFO which review work a cheaper checklist would already cover. |

## Engineering, assurance and change review

| Case and audience | What the walkthrough established | Where to test engagement with people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CorpOS** · billing on-call engineer, with a platform engineering lead as sponsor | The visitor can inspect synthetic service observations and the settled customer transaction before choosing an investigative handoff. One eligible vote leaves the restart pending. A second distinct vote triggers a gateway recheck. Rejecting the request leaves the $49 refund settled. At the pilot defaults, four minutes saved per case leaves a $180 shortfall and five breaks even. | Give the engineer a request with no service telemetry. Ask what they would check before casting a vote. Can the platform lead name an operational stop condition as well as a cost threshold? |
| **FidusGate** · incident investigator and gateway engineer, with a platform security lead as sponsor | The verifier accepts the signed denial and rejects a changed payload. Recording the finding, requesting the original packet and closing the ticket lead to different investigative dispositions. The investigator can then retrieve two repository revisions. Their blob IDs establish a content change during the interval. Attributing that change to this request would require additional records. | Give the investigator a different valid receipt and no execution log. Ask for one defensible sentence and the next evidence request. The sponsor should also ask how often receipts are missing when sampling starts from independent requests. |
| **HR-ERP** · HR systems owner, people operations and QA | The case opens on a passing approval. Both revisions show 72 hours. QA chooses further states to test and whether to sign off or hold. Feedback cites the evidence collected for that revision. With a declined request, deducting on submission still shows 72, whereas deducting on approval preserves the expected 80. A reviewer who tests only approval misses the defect. At the worksheet defaults, recovered capacity of $450 falls short of the $600 pilot. | Start QA at the passing approval case and ask them to find one additional test that could change sign-off. Ask people operations to explain the effect on the employee and the systems owner which cheaper check would catch it. |
| **SOC-2** · platform control owner and compliance lead responding to a customer’s vendor-risk reviewer | A settings snapshot can answer a configuration question. Linked review and merge records can instead support a claim about one release or expose a post-merge approval. Asking for evidence creates a task. A missing return leaves the claim open. Neutral quarter packets supply a delivery inventory, change register, selection rule and sampled approvals. The reviewer can find an omitted release or an incompletely documented approval before expanding the interpretation. A clean sample supports only that sample. | Give the control owner two review timestamps and the merge time. Ask for the customer response about that release and then about the quarter. Have the compliance lead weigh retrieval effort and missing evidence together. |

## How to run the participant check

Start with one operator and one prospective sponsor for a case. Give each a realistic task from the final column, without naming the control to use. Record the visitor’s initial prediction, the record they inspect, the action taken, their explanation of the consequence and any help they need. Then change one fact and ask them to repeat the decision. For the sponsor, change a cost or workload assumption and ask what evidence would reverse the funding recommendation. Observe whether visitors explore another path voluntarily. Completing the task, enjoying it and explaining it correctly are distinct observations.
