# Can visitors learn enough to make the next decision?

**Audience walkthrough, November 22, 2026.** Each of the ten proposed redesigns gives its operating user a decision to try and its prospective sponsor a reason to compare alternatives. The most revealing moments occur when one fact changes and the easy answer stops working. Examples include a report for the wrong referral, a leave request that HR declines, and a matching liquidity ratio calculated from different dates.

This assessment uses the rendered cases, project fixtures, desktop and mobile checks, and independent walkthroughs of the decision logic. No operators, customers or sponsors took part in a usability study. The findings below describe what the demos let people do and what to observe with participants. The [field guide](demo-field-guide.md) links the cases, screenshots and primary sources behind the audience choices.

## How to run the participant check

Start with one operator and one prospective sponsor for a case. Give each a realistic task from the final column, without naming the control to use. Record the visitor’s initial prediction, the record they inspect, the action taken, their explanation of the consequence and any help they need. Then change one fact and ask them to repeat the decision. For the sponsor, change a cost or workload assumption and ask what evidence would reverse the funding recommendation. Observe whether visitors explore another path voluntarily. Completing the task, enjoying it and explaining it correctly are distinct observations.
